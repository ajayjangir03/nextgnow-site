# ITU Agrees on 6G Performance Requirements in February 2026

Quick Summary

  • ITU-R reached consensus on IMT-2030 (6G) technical performance requirements in February 2026, representing the first major milestone toward 6G standardization
  • The agreed framework establishes target capabilities including peak data rates up to 1 Tbps, sub-millisecond latency, and energy efficiency improvements of 100x over 5G
  • Formal approval expected in December 2026 will trigger global harmonization of 6G specifications and enable 3GPP to finalize Release 21/22 standards for commercial deployment by 2030

Introduction

In February 2026, the International Telecommunication Union Radiocommunication Sector (ITU-R) achieved a historic milestone by reaching consensus on the technical performance requirements for IMT-2030, the official designation for sixth-generation (6G) wireless systems. This agreement, formalized through ITU-R Working Party 5D, establishes the foundational framework that will guide all subsequent 6G development efforts worldwide. The consensus document defines eight usage scenarios—expanding beyond 5G's three primary use cases—and specifies 15 key performance indicators (KPIs) that next-generation networks must achieve to qualify as IMT-2030 compliant.

This consensus represents the culmination of nearly four years of collaborative work among ITU member states, industry stakeholders, research institutions, and standards development organizations. The agreed performance requirements set ambitious targets that address emerging applications such as holographic communications, digital twin synchronization, extended reality (XR) with haptic feedback, and massive autonomous system coordination. While formal approval is scheduled for December 2026 at the ITU-R plenary session, the February consensus enables equipment manufacturers, chipset vendors, and network operators to accelerate their 6G research and development with clarity on technical objectives.

The timing of this agreement is particularly significant as it occurs during the mature deployment phase of 5G-Advanced (3GPP Releases 18-19) and coincides with initial 6G research trials being conducted in testbeds across North America, Europe, and Asia-Pacific. The ITU framework will directly inform 3GPP's work on Releases 21 and 22, expected to contain the first complete 6G specifications, ensuring global interoperability and harmonized spectrum utilization for services anticipated to launch commercially between 2029 and 2031.

Why This Concept Matters

The ITU-R consensus on 6G performance requirements represents far more than a procedural milestone—it establishes the technical foundation upon which the next decade of telecommunications innovation will be built. For network operators, this agreement provides crucial planning certainty for infrastructure investments that will exceed hundreds of billions of dollars globally. Operators can now confidently develop network evolution roadmaps, spectrum acquisition strategies, and business cases for 6G services, knowing the technical goalposts are firmly established. Without this international consensus, fragmentation across regional implementations would increase costs, reduce economies of scale, and delay global roaming capabilities.

For telecommunications engineers and technology developers, the agreed performance requirements translate into concrete design parameters for radio access technologies, core network architectures, and edge computing platforms. Engineers now have validated targets for critical specifications: peak data rates of 1 Tbps downlink and 500 Gbps uplink, user-experienced data rates of 1 Gbps everywhere, connection densities reaching 10 million devices per square kilometer, positioning accuracy to 1 centimeter, and energy efficiency improvements delivering 100 times better performance per watt than 5G. These specifications drive innovation in semiconductor design, antenna technology, signal processing algorithms, and network automation systems.

The broader telecommunications industry benefits from the certainty this consensus creates across the value chain. Equipment manufacturers can finalize product development timelines and allocate R&D resources with confidence. Spectrum regulators gain the technical foundation to plan World Radiocommunication Conference 2027 (WRC-27) decisions regarding 6G frequency allocations. Vertical industries—including automotive, manufacturing, healthcare, and entertainment—can begin developing applications that leverage 6G's unique capabilities, knowing these performance characteristics will be globally standardized rather than proprietary implementations.

Perhaps most importantly, the ITU consensus ensures that 6G development includes considerations beyond raw performance metrics. The agreed framework explicitly addresses sustainability targets, digital inclusion objectives, and security-by-design principles. This holistic approach reflects lessons learned from 5G deployment challenges and positions 6G as not merely an incremental performance upgrade but a transformative platform that addresses societal needs alongside technical capabilities. The February 2026 consensus thus establishes 6G as a technology designed to support sustainable development goals while delivering unprecedented connectivity performance.

Architecture / Components Involved

ITU-R Working Party 5D

The primary standards body within the ITU responsible for developing IMT specifications, including performance requirements, evaluation methodologies, and spectrum considerations. WP 5D coordinates input from ITU member states and maintains liaison relationships with regional and global standards organizations including 3GPP, IEEE, ETSI, and industry forums.

IMT-2030 Framework Document

The formal specification document (ITU-R M.2160 series) that defines the vision, usage scenarios, and key performance indicators for 6G systems. This framework establishes the technical baseline against which all candidate radio interface technologies and network architectures will be evaluated during the IMT-2030 submission and evaluation process.

Eight Usage Scenarios

Expanded from 5G's three scenarios (eMBB, URLLC, mMTC), the 6G framework includes: Immersive Communication (holographic and multisensory experiences), Hyper-Reliable Low-Latency Communication (mission-critical systems with 99.99999% reliability), Ubiquitous Connectivity (global coverage including air, maritime, and space), Integrated Sensing and Communication (simultaneous radar and communication), Massive IoT Evolution (10M+ devices/km²), Extreme Data Rates (multi-Tbps applications), Sustainable Connectivity (energy-efficient networks), and AI-Native Networks (embedded machine learning).

Fifteen Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

Quantifiable metrics including: peak data rate (1 Tbps DL, 500 Gbps UL), user-experienced data rate (1 Gbps minimum), latency (0.1-1 ms depending on scenario), reliability (up to 99.99999%), connection density (10M devices/km²), mobility (up to 1,000 km/h), energy efficiency (100x improvement), spectrum efficiency (3x improvement over 5G), area traffic capacity (100 Mbps/m²), positioning accuracy (10 cm horizontal, 1 cm vertical), sensing resolution (1 cm range, 1 cm/s velocity), coverage (global including NTN), security level (quantum-resistant cryptography), intelligence level (autonomous network operations), and sustainability index (carbon footprint metrics).

3GPP Coordination Interface

The liaison mechanism through which ITU-R requirements are translated into detailed 3GPP technical specifications. This interface ensures that 3GPP Releases 21 and 22 (the first 6G releases) align with IMT-2030 requirements and that radio interface technologies (RIT) and radio transmission technologies (RTT) submitted for IMT-2030 evaluation are based on 3GPP specifications.

Regional Standardization Bodies

Organizations including ETSI (Europe), ATIS/TIA (North America), ARIB/TTC (Japan), CCSA (China), TSDSI (India), and TTA (Korea) that contribute regional perspectives to ITU requirements and ensure alignment between ITU-R specifications and regional regulatory frameworks. These bodies coordinate national research initiatives and testbed results that informed the February 2026 consensus.

Evaluation Groups

Independent technical evaluation bodies that will assess candidate IMT-2030 technologies against the agreed requirements. These groups, operating under ITU-R procedures, will conduct simulation studies and validation testing between 2027 and 2029 to determine which submitted technologies meet IMT-2030 criteria for official designation.

Spectrum Allocation Framework

The frequency planning component that identifies candidate bands for 6G deployment, including mid-band spectrum (7-24 GHz), millimeter-wave expansion (above 100 GHz), and potential use of sub-THz frequencies (92-300 GHz). The performance requirements directly inform spectrum needs calculations that will guide WRC-27 and WRC-31 decisions.

How It Works — Step by Step

Step 1: Requirements Development Process (2022-2025)

ITU-R Working Party 5D initiated the IMT-2030 requirements development process in 2022, following the completion of IMT-2020 (5G) evaluation. Member states, industry participants, and research organizations submitted contributions proposing usage scenarios, KPI definitions, and target values based on market forecasts for the 2030-2040 timeframe. WP 5D conducted iterative review cycles, harmonizing divergent proposals and resolving technical disagreements through consensus-building discussions. By late 2025, a stable draft framework emerged incorporating input from over 100 submitted contributions.

Step 2: Consensus Building in Working Groups (January-February 2026)

During the January 2026 WP 5D meeting, delegations reviewed the consolidated draft requirements document, identifying remaining areas of contention—primarily concerning feasibility of certain performance targets, trade-offs between competing KPIs, and sustainability measurement methodologies. Intensive technical discussions occurred in ad-hoc groups focused on specific topics: radio interface parameters, network performance metrics, energy efficiency calculations, and evaluation methodologies. Through compromise and technical analysis, delegates converged on balanced requirements that were ambitious yet technically achievable within the 2030 deployment timeframe.

Step 3: Formal Consensus Agreement (February 2026)

On February 14, 2026, WP 5D achieved formal consensus on the complete IMT-2030 framework document, including all usage scenarios, KPI definitions, target values, and evaluation procedures. This consensus required agreement among all participating administrations (representing ITU member states) and liaison organizations. The consensus document was captured in draft Recommendation ITU-R M.2160-TEMP, with temporary designation pending final approval. The agreement triggered notifications to 3GPP, IEEE, and other partner organizations to align their work programs with the established requirements.

Step 4: Public Review and Comment Period (March-November 2026)

Following consensus, the draft Recommendation enters a public review period where ITU member states consult with national stakeholders, industry groups conduct technical validation studies, and any final objections or clarifications are submitted to ITU-R. This period allows broader validation of the requirements' technical feasibility and market relevance. During this phase, 3GPP began mapping IMT-2030 requirements to Release 21 work items, and research institutions initiated simulation studies to validate achievability of specified performance targets.

Step 5: Final Approval and Publication (December 2026)

The ITU-R Study Group 5 plenary session scheduled for December 2026 will conduct final review and formal approval of Recommendation ITU-R M.2160 containing the IMT-2030 framework. This approval converts the consensus agreement into an official ITU Recommendation with international recognition. Upon publication, the Recommendation becomes the authoritative reference for all subsequent 6G development activities, including 3GPP standardization, regional regulatory decisions, and the IMT-2030 technology submission and evaluation process scheduled for 2027-2029.

Step 6: Translation to 3GPP Requirements (2027)

Following ITU-R approval, 3GPP working groups (particularly RAN, SA, and CT) formally incorporate IMT-2030 requirements into Release 21 and 22 work item descriptions. This translation process converts ITU's scenario-based requirements into specific technical specifications for radio protocols, network architectures, and service capabilities. 3GPP conducts detailed feasibility studies and develops candidate technologies—including advanced MIMO systems, AI-integrated protocols, integrated sensing techniques, and quantum-resistant security—designed to meet or exceed ITU requirements.

Step 7: Industry Implementation and Validation (2027-2029)

Equipment vendors and operators begin developing prototype systems based on the ITU requirements and emerging 3GPP specifications. Industry testbeds validate performance claims through measurement campaigns, demonstrating achievability of key metrics such as peak data rates, latency targets, and energy efficiency goals. These validation results feed back into the formal IMT-2030 evaluation process, where independent evaluation groups assess whether submitted technologies meet the agreed requirements for official IMT-2030 designation.

Step 8: IMT-2030 Technology Evaluation and Recognition (2028-2030)

Organizations submit complete radio interface technology and transmission technology proposals to ITU-R, claiming compliance with IMT-2030 requirements. Evaluation groups conduct rigorous assessment using standardized methodologies defined in the December 2026 Recommendation. Technologies that successfully meet all mandatory requirements receive official IMT-2030 designation, enabling them to be marketed as "6G compliant" and ensuring global regulatory acceptance. This formal recognition, expected between late 2029 and 2030, enables commercial deployment of 6G networks and services.

Technical Deep Dive

The ITU-R M.2160 series Recommendation framework for IMT-2030 establishes technical requirements across multiple dimensions, building upon and significantly extending the IMT-2020 (5G) foundation defined in ITU-R M.2083 and M.2410. The February 2026 consensus crystallizes four years of technical analysis into quantifiable specifications that push the boundaries of wireless system performance.

Performance Requirement Specifications

The peak data rate requirement of 1 Tbps downlink and 500 Gbps uplink represents a 10-50x improvement over 5G's peak rates. This necessitates innovations across multiple technical domains: ultra-wide bandwidth channels (potentially 2 GHz continuous or aggregated bandwidth), advanced modulation schemes (likely beyond 4096-QAM), massive MIMO configurations (potentially 512+ antenna elements), and spatial multiplexing exploiting 3D beamforming in both sub-THz and mmWave frequencies. The asymmetry in DL/UL rates reflects realistic application profiles while acknowledging uplink power constraints in mobile devices.

User-experienced data rate targets of 1 Gbps minimum—compared to 100 Mbps in 5G—ensure ubiquitous high-quality service rather than merely peak performance in optimal conditions. This requirement drives network densification strategies, advanced interference management techniques, and seamless mobility across heterogeneous network layers including terrestrial, aerial, and satellite components. The specification implies cell edge performance improvements through coordinated multi-point (CoMP) evolution, intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRS), and AI-optimized resource allocation.

Latency requirements span multiple service classes: 0.1 ms for haptic communication and industrial automation, 0.5 ms for extended reality applications, 1 ms for immersive communication, and 10 ms for traditional mobile broadband. These targets require fundamental architectural changes beyond 5G's user plane latency of 1-4 ms, including: sub-slot transmission structures (potentially 1-symbol TTI), edge-native computing with in-network processing, preemptive scheduling algorithms, and deterministic networking mechanisms aligned with IEEE TSN and DetNet standards.

Reliability specifications reaching 99.99999% (10^-7 failure rate) for critical services exceed 5G URLLC targets by two orders of magnitude. Achieving this requires redundancy mechanisms including: multi-connectivity with independent failure domains, proactive rerouting based on predictive analytics, packet duplication across diverse paths, and time-sensitive networking integration. The specification acknowledges that different service classes require different reliability-latency profiles, with the framework defining specific combinations for each usage scenario.

Spectrum and Radio Technology Parameters

The IMT-2030 framework anticipates operation across an unprecedented frequency range, from legacy sub-6 GHz bands through mmWave (24-100 GHz) to sub-THz frequencies (100-300 GHz). This spectrum diversity enables the massive bandwidth aggregation necessary for Tbps peak rates while maintaining coverage and mobility through multi-band operation. The framework specifies spectrum efficiency targets of 30 bps/Hz downlink and 15 bps/Hz uplink (3x improvement over 5G), achievable through advanced MIMO techniques including: holographic beamforming, orbital angular momentum multiplexing, and full-duplex operation enabled by self-interference cancellation exceeding 120 dB.

Channel models defined in the accompanying ITU-R M.2412 extension for IMT-2030 evaluation include new propagation scenarios: indoor hotspot at sub-THz frequencies (100-300 GHz), non-terrestrial network channels for LEO/MEO/GEO satellites, urban micro with intelligent reflecting surfaces, and high-speed train scenarios up to 1,000 km/h. These models incorporate frequency-dependent atmospheric absorption, blockage dynamics from mobile objects, and time-varying characteristics of reconfigurable intelligent surfaces.

Network Architecture Requirements

The consensus framework mandates an "AI-native" architecture where machine learning is embedded at every network layer rather than added as an overlay. This requires standardization of: training data collection interfaces, model distribution mechanisms, inference latency guarantees, and explainability frameworks for regulatory compliance. The architecture must support distributed learning across edge nodes, federated learning preserving privacy, and online learning adapting to dynamic conditions—capabilities being developed in 3GPP SA2's Release 21 work on AIML for Network Automation.

Integrated sensing and communication (ISAC) represents a fundamental architectural change where the same waveforms and infrastructure serve both communication and radar/positioning functions. The framework specifies sensing capabilities including: 1 cm range resolution, 1 cm/s velocity resolution, 10 cm positioning accuracy, and 1° angle resolution. This dual functionality impacts waveform design (favoring OFDM derivatives with enhanced autocorrelation properties), frame structure (allocating resources for sensing reference signals), and processing architecture (joint communication-sensing signal processing).

The sustainability requirements embedded in the framework mandate 100x improvement in network energy efficiency (measured in bits transmitted per Joule consumed) compared to 5G baselines. This drives architectural innovations including: sleep mode operations with sub-millisecond wake-up, energy harvesting integration for low-power devices, AI-driven traffic prediction enabling proactive resource scaling, and cell-free architectures minimizing transmit power through proximity to users. The framework establishes measurement methodologies aligned with ETSI ES 203 228 for consistent energy efficiency evaluation.

Evaluation Methodology and Compliance

The consensus document defines rigorous evaluation procedures that candidate IMT-2030 technologies must undergo for official recognition. Evaluation groups will conduct link-level and system-level simulations using standardized parameters: traffic models (including new holographic and sensory communication patterns), deployment scenarios (from dense urban to rural and NTN), and baseline configurations (antenna configurations, power levels, receiver architectures). Technologies must demonstrate compliance across all mandatory requirements while evaluation groups verify claims through independent implementation of proposed solutions.

The framework introduces probabilistic requirements where appropriate, acknowledging that absolute guarantees across all conditions are technically infeasible. For example, the 1 Gbps user-experienced rate must be achieved for 95% of users in 95% of locations, allowing for statistical variations while ensuring consistently high performance. This approach, refined from lessons learned in IMT-2020 evaluation, provides realistic targets while maintaining technical rigor.

Interface with 3GPP Standardization

The ITU-R requirements directly inform 3GPP's work on Release 21 (targeted for completion in 2028) and Release 22 (2029), which will contain the first complete 6G specifications. The mapping process translates ITU's scenario-based requirements into detailed technical specifications across 3GPP working groups:

  • RAN1/RAN2: Physical layer and MAC protocols to achieve data rate, latency, and reliability targets
  • RAN3: Network architecture including IAB evolution, integrated access and backhaul for NTN, and multi-RAT coordination
  • SA1/SA2: Service requirements, architecture frameworks, and procedures for new capabilities like ISAC and AI-native operations
  • SA3: Security architecture incorporating quantum-resistant cryptography and privacy-preserving AI
  • SA5: Management requirements for zero-touch operations and energy efficiency optimization
  • CT1/CT4: Core network protocols, including evolution of 5GC to support 6G services and non-terrestrial network integration

The liaison process ensures bidirectional feedback: 3GPP informs ITU of technical feasibility findings during requirement development, while ITU provides high-level framework guidance that prevents 3GPP specification fragmentation. This coordination has proven effective through IMT-2000, IMT-Advanced, and IMT-2020 generations and continues for IMT-2030.

Real-World Example / Use Case

South Korea's K-Network 2030 Initiative: Industrial IoT Testbed Implementation

Following the February 2026 ITU consensus, South Korea's Ministry of Science and ICT accelerated its K-Network 2030 program by deploying a 6G industrial testbed at the Hyundai Motor manufacturing complex in Ulsan. This implementation demonstrates how ITU-R requirements translate into practical system design and validates the technical feasibility of key IMT-2030 specifications.

The testbed deployment addresses the "Hyper-Reliable Low-Latency Communication" and "Massive IoT Evolution" scenarios from the ITU framework, supporting smart manufacturing applications including: robotic assembly with haptic feedback, digital twin synchronization of production lines, autonomous material transport vehicles, and quality inspection using integrated sensing capabilities.
